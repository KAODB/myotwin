How it works

An assumed sensor panel in the sleep station. Nothing is worn, nothing to remember.

Impedance strip under the calves

Dry electrodes in the mattress pass a tiny 50 kHz current through the calf. The phase angle falls as muscle is lost, so a nightly reading tracks muscle without anyone doing a test.

60 GHz radar above the bunk

A low-power radar reads chest movement from a distance: breathing rate, heart rate, sleep time and restlessness. No contact, no camera.

Particle trackers above and below

Tracker planes record the path of each cosmic-ray particle passing through the sleeper. That gives the night's dose, the direction it came from, and, summed over many tracks, a density image of the body.

What this version does not account for, and the next step

  • All data are simulated. No sensor in this design has been built or flown.
  • The link between calf impedance phase and muscle loss is an assumption based on cast and bed-rest studies on Earth, not on spaceflight data.
  • Bed-integrated dry-electrode impedance has not been studied; contact and fluid shifts after lying down can move the reading.
  • Radar vital signs have not been tested in space.
  • Dose is absorbed dose in silicon over the sleep period, not effective dose to the person.
  • The body image comes from a simplified phantom on a 2 cm grid.
  • Thresholds are interim and unvalidated.

Next step: run the panel alongside standard tests in a ground bed-rest study, and fit the thresholds to the measured night-to-night noise.
