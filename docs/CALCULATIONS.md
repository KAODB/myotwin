# How every number is calculated

Generated by `node tools/calculations-md.js > docs/CALCULATIONS.md`; do not edit by hand, `test/docs.test.js` fails when it is out of date.
Written with an SI tool (Claude) and reviewed by the team.

This page walks through each number the MyoTwin page shows. Parameters, thresholds and example values are read from the code and data files when the page is generated; the explanations around them are written by hand. Ids such as (V10) are entries in [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md). "Assumption" means a modelling choice the code declares as one.

## 1. What is measured, what is simulated, what is assumed

| Reading | Source in this version | Where it would come from in the bunk |
|---|---|---|
| Calf impedance phase at 50 kHz | Simulated from the muscle model | Impedance strip under the calves (assumed) |
| Hours asleep, heart and breathing rate | Simulated | 60 GHz radar above the bunk (assumed) |
| Radiation dose per night | Measured, replayed: Curiosity RAD on Mars (V60), LRO CRaTER in lunar orbit (V61) | Particle trackers above and below the sleeper (assumed) |
| Solar particle event on a night | NASA DONKI records (V62) | Space-weather alerts and a proton monitor |
| Proton flux chart | NASA OMNI2 hourly data (V63) | Space-weather data |
| Particle arrival directions | Simulated | Particle trackers |
| Body image | Simulated from a body phantom | Trackers, pressure mat, impedance strip |
| Exercise done or missed | Simulated | Resistance-exercise log |
| Morning check-in | Entered by the person at the screen | The crew member |

Each night gives one record (fields and units in [CONTRACT.md](CONTRACT.md)); its `_truth` part holds simulated values that only the body image reads. The simulated nights and the mission plan come from the same model, so the page shows the workflow; it cannot show how well a real decline would be detected.

## 2. Muscle

The simulated calf muscle change, in percent, moves each day towards a target:

```
target = -100 * Lmax * (1 - g)^p * (1 - eff * adh)
m[d+1] = m[d] + (target - m[d]) * (1 - exp(-1 / tau))     while g < 1
m[d+1] = m[d] * exp(-1 / tau_r)                            while g >= 1
```

`m[d]` is the change on day d (0 at the start); `g` is gravity as a fraction of Earth's (Moon 0.165, Mars 0.38, transit 0) (V14); `adh` is exercise adherence, 0 to 1.

| Symbol | Parameter | Value | Unit | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lmax | maxLossFraction | 0.38 | fraction of calf muscle volume | fitted | V4, V5 |
| tau | timeConstantDays | 42 | days | fitted | V4, V5 |
| eff | exerciseEfficacy | 0.72 | fraction of loss prevented at full adherence | fitted | V4, V5 |
| tau_r | recoveryTimeConstantDays | 30 | days | assumption |  |
| p | gravityShape | 1 | exponent | assumption | V6, V90, V91 |
| q | eimPhasePctPerMusclePct | 1 | % phase change per % muscle change | assumption | V8 |

**The fit.** Lmax, tau and eff were chosen together. At g = 0 the model gives -28.9% after 60 days of bed rest without exercise (published -29%), -8.1% with exercise (published -8%) (V4), and -10.5% after 180 days on the ISS with exercise (published -13 +/- 2%) (V5), a smaller loss than published. The fit treats head-down bed rest as g = 0 and the ISS crews as fully adherent. A night's exercise is logged as done when a uniform random draw is below the adherence.

**What-if values.** Besides exercise adherence, the page address can set the gravity shape p, the shielding share sh (section 7) and the impedance noise sdP. Changing p changes the mission plan as well as the readings; changing adherence changes only the readings.

**The impedance reading.**

```
phase = max(0, P0 * (1 + q * m / 100)) * (1 + f / 100) * (1 + sdP * z)
```

`P0` is the person's phase before departure (assumption: Moon base 9.2, Mars 8.8, solar event 9.5 degrees; compare 9.46 degrees in V8); `q` = 1; `f` = -1% on Moon and Mars nights and 0 on Earth, an offset for the changed fluid distribution whose size is assumed (bioimpedance is fluid-sensitive: in V28 leg bioimpedance reached no steady state within 30 minutes of lying down); `sdP` = 0.02, the night-to-night spread (assumption; operator-applied impedance varies by 4.2% between sessions, V10); `z` is a standard normal draw. Stored to 3 decimals. The checks compare the phase with the person's own baseline phase in percent; they never turn it back into muscle.

**Baseline, window and plan.** The baseline is the median `B` and sample standard deviation `SD` of the phase over the 14 nights before departure that have a usable scan (at least 3). `M7` is the median phase of the nights with a usable scan among the last 7 nights (at least 3); windows never mix baseline and surface nights. The plan is the same model run with the scenario as intended (on Mars, without the 21-day exercise lapse); `expectedDrop` is the median of minus the plan's change over the same usable-scan nights.

```
drop    = (B - M7) / B * 100
noise7  = 1.2533 * SD / sqrt(7) / B * 100
watch   = max(2 * noise7, 4.2)
report  = max(3 * noise7, 16.8)
```

`noise7` is the expected spread of a 7-night median in percent (1.2533 = sqrt(pi/2), an assumption). 4.2% and 16.8% are the average and largest test-retest variability of the measurement (V10); the multiples are assumptions. **Report** when `drop > report`; otherwise **Watch** when `drop - expectedDrop > watch`; otherwise **In range**.

**Projection.** When the check is not on Report, a least-squares line is fitted to up to 4 complete weekly medians (at least 3, consecutive surface weeks back from tonight, in percent from baseline). With a negative slope the page shows it and `days = round((-report - now) / slope * 7)`, `now` being the line's value tonight; it says "within about a week" up to 7 days. It is left out when days is 0 or less, above 365, or the slope rounds to zero at 1 decimal.

**Worked example: Mars, night 42.** B = 8.847 degrees, SD 0.197 (14 nights); noise7 = 1.06%, so watch = 4.2% and report = 16.8%. Usable nights 36 to 42: 7.389, 7.44, 7.493, 7.433, 7.286, 7.235, 7.286; M7 = 7.389. drop = 16.5% (the page shows -16.5%); expectedDrop = 9.1%. drop is below report; drop - expectedDrop = 7.3 points, above watch. Status: **Watch**. Projection: -1.1% per week, 5 days, "within about a week". (Simulated true value, unread by the check: -15.6%.)

## 3. Checking the gravity term against NASA rat data

The gravity term is set beside rats kept for 28 days at 40% weight bearing, fully unloaded or at full weight bearing (NASA OSDR, V90, V91; `data/osdr-pwb.js`):

```
change(x) = 100 * (mean on day 28 / mean on day 0 - 1)
share     = (change(0.4 g) - change(1 g)) / (change(0 g) - change(1 g))
```

`mean` is the group mean; the 1 g change removes growth effects. Shares: grip force females 0.66, grip force males 0.93, plantarflexion torque females 0.46, plantarflexion torque males 0.25, calf girth females 1.05, calf girth males 0.44.

Minimum 0.25, median 0.56, maximum 1.05. The model's share at 0.4 g is (1 - 0.4)^1 = 0.60. The linear assumption sits near the middle of a wide spread. The rats are an analog, and strength and girth are not muscle volume. A plausibility check of one assumption, not a fit.

## 4. Sleep

```
sleep_h = clamp(mu + 0.4 * z, 2, 11)
```

`mu` is the scenario mean (assumption: 7.5 h on the Moon, 7.4 h on Mars, 5.5 h on nights 30 to 36 of the Moon base scenario); `z` a standard normal draw. Stored to 2 decimals.

The check takes the mean of the nights with radar data among the last 7 (at least 4). **Watch** below 7 h, the low end of the 7 to 9 hours an average adult needs (V79). **Report** below 6 h, the ISS average of 6.09 h rounded down (V11); an assumption that has not been validated as a cut-off.

**Worked example: Moon base, night 36.** 5.96, 5.85, 5.31, 4.99, 5.5, 6.38, 5.06 h; mean 5.58 h, below 6 h and below 7 h. Status: **Report**; the card shows 5.6 h.

## 5. Heart and breathing

```
hr_bpm = clamp(HR0 + dHR + 2 * z, 35, 130)
rr_bpm = clamp(RR0 + dRR + 0.5 * z, 8, 30)
limit  = baseline median + 2 * baseline SD
```

`HR0`, `RR0` are the person's levels (assumption: Moon base 52 and 13.5, Mars 56 and 14.5, solar event 50 and 13); `dHR`, `dRR` are 9 beats and 3 breaths per minute on the illness nights 60 to 63 on Mars, otherwise 0. The baseline is the median and sample SD of the 14 nights before departure. The check takes the median of the nights with radar data among the last 3 (at least 2). **Watch** when either median is above its limit; when both rates are above their limits on each of the last 3 nights, it stays on Watch with a text that names illness. The code has no Report level here: the contract treats a raised rate as a reason to check for illness.

**Worked example: Mars, night 62.** Heart 62.3, 63.9, 66.1, median 63.9, above the limit 60.7; breathing 18.8, 18.1, 17.3, median 18.1, above the limit 15.4. Status: **Watch**, sustained; the card shows 64 bpm.

## 6. Radiation

```
Mars: dose = sol median dose rate (uGy/h) * 8 h
Moon: dose = sum over the 8 hours from 22:00 UTC of (rate in cGy/day * 1e4 / 24)
```

**Mars** (`data/msl-rad.js`, Curiosity RAD detector B, dose in silicon, V60): mission night k, counting from 0 at arrival, uses row k of the file, counting from 0; the file holds the median dose rate of each of 94 sols between 13 and 112 (sols 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 64 have no data and are skipped). The 90 Mars nights use sols 13 to 108; 8 h is the assumed sleep period. Night 42: row 42, sol 60 (2012-10-06), 8.923 µGy/h x 8 = 71.4 µGy.

**Moon** (`data/lro-crater.js`, LRO CRaTER pair D5&6, scaled by the instrument team from lunar orbit to the surface and corrected to water, V61): the hourly rates summed over the fixed window 22:00-06:00 UTC. The Moon base scenario replays 2017-04-21 to 2017-06-19; the solar-event scenario starts at 2017-08-21, so night 20 is 2017-09-10; the windows are this project's choice.

**Rules.** The usual night is the median dose of up to 30 earlier surface nights (No data with fewer than 7). **Watch** above 2 times the usual night; **Report** above 10 times, or whenever a solar particle event is on record. The multiples are assumptions. The running total sums every surface night's dose up to tonight.

**Solar particle events** (solar-event scenario only, V62): a night is marked when a DONKI record from a GOES instrument starts between 16 h before the sleep window opens and the end of the window (8 h after it opens). DONKI records cover 2017-09-01 to 2017-10-01 only; nights whose look-up span leaves that range are not checked.

**Proton flux chart** (solar-event scenario only, V63): OMNI2 hourly flux above 10 MeV near Earth, 2017-09-04 to 2017-09-16, as given, on a log axis labelled "Protons >10 MeV (pfu)" from 0.1 to 10000, with the selected night's 8-hour sleep window shaded. **Directions** are simulated: 100 counts a night, 30% more in one assumed sector; the page sums them over the mission.

**Shielding.** The dose is not reduced for habitat shielding. For cosmic rays on quiet nights the code gives two reasons: over the 60 quiet nights the medians of the two CRaTER pairs at opposite ends of the telescope differ by 1.9% (V61), and NASA's guidance recommends about 20 g/cm2 of hydrogen-rich shielding, beyond which more does not help against cosmic rays (V37). Solar protons behave differently: on 2017-09-10 the Moon-facing pair read 0.26 times the thinly shielded pair (V61). That pair is a lightly shielded detector on an orbiter (V93), not a model of a habitat. Thick habitat shielding stops most solar protons, so a crew inside would receive much less. Under 23 g/cm2 of Martian air, Curiosity's RAD saw the dose rate rise only 2- to 3-fold in the same event (V92).

**Worked example: solar event, night 20 (2017-09-10).** 43538.8 µGy; usual night 86.3 µGy (median of 20 earlier nights); ratio 504.5, above 10. DONKI records 2017-09-10T16:25:00-SEP-001 and 2017-09-10T16:45:00-SEP-001 (GOES) fall inside the look-up span. Status: **Report** on both counts. Running total 48499.9 µGy. The page shows doses from 1000 µGy in mGy to one decimal and ratios from 100 as whole numbers: 43.5 mGy, 505x the usual level, total 48.5 mGy. EVIDENCE.md gives 486 times for the same night because V61 compares with the median of the 60-night quiet window (89.6 µGy), while the check uses the earlier nights of this scenario.

## 7. The body image

All three layers are simulated from a body phantom: a supine adult on 32 x 96 cells of 2 cm (sizes assumed), giving each cell a path length through soft tissue and bone and a region. Muscle change thins calf and thigh tissue:

```
soft = soft0 * (1 + fm * m / 100)
```

`fm` is the muscle share (assumption: calf 0.7, thigh 0.6); `m` is the simulated change from `_truth`; the thigh uses m x 0.72, the thigh-to-calf loss ratio without exercise in V4. An image built over several nights uses the mean of m over those nights.

**Density** (experimental):

```
N     = I * 2 * pi * (1 - cos(theta)) * H * 3600 * sh
x/X0  = soft / X0_muscle + bone / X0_bone
value = x/X0 * (1 + M / sqrt(N * Acell) * z)
```

`N` is tracks per cm2; `I` = 0.2 per cm2 per s per sr (assumption inside the range implied by V33); `theta` = 20 degrees; `H` = 8 h; `sh` = 1, the share of tracks crossing the shielding (what-if value). One night gives N = 2183. `x/X0` is areal density in radiation lengths, the legend's quantity (X0 = 35.14 cm muscle, 16.48 cm compact bone, V31). `M` = 5 stands for the unknown particle momentum (assumption); `Acell` = 4 cm2; one night's relative noise is 5.4%. Cells outside the body get the noise of 2 cm of muscle around 0. On a quiet night N is scaled by the night's dose over the scenario median. A night is left out when a solar particle event is on record or its dose exceeds 1.15 times the scenario median, because solar protons stop in the body or cross it far slower (V61, V62). The change view sums N over the usable nights among the last 7 surface nights.

**Pressure:** `kPa = (soft * rho_soft + bone * rho_bone) * 10 * g0 * ratio / 1000`, with `rho_soft` = 1.04 and `rho_bone` = 1.85 g/cm3 (V31), 10 for g/cm2 to kg/m2, `g0` = 9.80665 m/s2 and `ratio` the world's gravity ratio (V14); each column of tissue rests on the cell below it (assumption).

**Impedance:** calf cells carry the night's phase (the median of the window's nights in the change view); no other region is measured, so the outline of the other regions gets hollow dots and their interior stays empty.

**Dots.** Dot area follows the value: the radius runs from 0.16 to 0.50 of a cell by the square root of the scaled value. Raw scales run from 0 to the image maximum (density, pressure) and from 0.75 to 1.05 times the baseline phase (impedance).

```
change(cell)   = (now / reference - 1) * 100
change(region) = mean of change(cell) over the region
range          = max(10, ceil(largest |change(region)|))
noise(region)  = M * sqrt(1 / (N * Acell * n)) * sqrt(2) * 100
```

`now` is the image of the last 7 surface nights, `reference` that of the first 7 (density: usable nights only, seed 7); in the noise formula `N` is the summed tracks of the window and `n` the cells in the region; sqrt(2) is for the difference of two images. Blue dots are a loss, white a gain; in the density view a region below 2 x noise gets small grey dots. Hollow outline dots mark what has nothing to show: outside the calves for impedance, the whole body for density when every night of the week is left out.

## 8. The morning check-in

The person enters mood (1 to 5), temperature (34 to 42 °C) and any of legs weaker or sore, not rested and feeling ill.

```
rise  = (round(T * 10) - round(B * 10)) / 10
fever = round(rise * 10) > round(1.1 * 10)
low   = round(-rise * 10) > round(1.1 * 10)
```

`T` is the temperature entered; `B` = 36.6 °C, the person's baseline (assumption). The rise limit is the rise that counts as fever in older adults in long-term care (V97), adapted here for a crew; the drop limit mirrors it (assumption). Mood 2 or lower is low (assumption). Flags, in order: fever, low temperature, feeling ill (only without fever), low mood, legs, not rested. Any flag puts the check-in on **Watch**; it has no Report level. The meaning is the first flag's sentence, then "Also noted:" and the rest; actions follow the flags without repeats, at most 5. Each body check is set beside its answer (legs, not rested, ill or fever): both clear, both show a change, only the person, only the bunk, or no bunk reading. Radiation cannot be felt.

**Worked example: Mars, night 62, mood 2, 38.2 °C, legs and ill.** rise = (382 - 366) / 10 = 1.6 °C, above the limit: fever. Flags: fever, mood, legs (feeling ill is not a separate flag when there is a fever). Status **Watch**, 3 flags, 5 actions. Comparison: muscle only you; sleep agree; cardio both show a change; radiation cannot be felt.

## 9. From statuses to words

The headline names checks on Report first ("Report to the flight surgeon today: ..."), then on Watch ("N check(s) to watch: ..."); otherwise checks with No data, or all four in range. The three step cards count the checks on Watch or Report, the check-in flags and the actions in the Today list. The Today list has one entry for each check on Report, then on Watch, then the check-in when it is on Watch.

Mars night 42: "1 check to watch: muscle." Step cards "1 check needs attention", "Not done yet · 30 seconds", "3 things to do". With the check-in of section 8 on night 62: "1 check to watch: heart and breathing." Step cards "1 check needs attention", "Done · 3 things flagged", "7 things to do"; Today: Heart and breathing (2 actions), Your check-in (5 actions).

## 10. Reproducibility

The simulator is seeded (Moon base 11, Mars 23, solar event 37) and draws every random number every night, and the page keeps scenario, night, what-if values and check-in in its address, so the same address gives the same numbers. Tests recompute the evidence numbers, the gravity-check shares, the per-night doses and the fitted results from the data files: `node --test "test/*.test.js"`.
