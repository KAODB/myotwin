- Source: arXiv:1609.09442 (2016), citing McKinney et al. 2006; the original was not opened. <https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09442>
- Quote: "integral 4pi-steradian flux of galactic cosmic ray ("GCR") protons varies from 1.5 to 5.0 particles/cm2/s over the course of the 11-year solar cycle (McKinney et al. 2006)"
- Values: `fluxMinPerCm2S` = 1.5; `fluxMaxPerCm2S` = 5

**V34**. Sea-level cosmic-ray muons: about 70 per m2 per second per steradian vertically; about 1 per cm2 per minute through a horizontal detector.

- Status: verified (reference-table)
- Source: Particle Data Group, Cosmic Rays review (2022). <https://pdg.lbl.gov/2022/reviews/rpp2022-rev-cosmic-rays.pdf>
- Quote: "The integral intensity of vertical muons above 1 GeV/c at sea level is ~ 70 m^-2 s^-1 sr^-1 ... I ~ 1 cm^-2 min^-1 for horizontal detectors."
- Values: `verticalPerM2SSr` = 70; `horizontalPerCm2Min` = 1

**V35**. Sea-level cosmic-ray muons have been used to image an anthropomorphic phantom: a 24-hour exposure with signal to background of about 1:1.

- Status: verified (preprint)
- Source: Morris CL et al. arXiv:2106.01542; J Appl Phys 2025, doi 10.1063/5.0253548. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01542>
- Quote: "A 24-hour exposure was sufficient ... the signal to background was ~1:1."
- Values: `exposureHours` = 24

**V36**. Large silicon-strip trackers already fly in space: Fermi LAT has 16 towers of 18 x/y planes with 228 um strip pitch.

- Status: verified (full-text)
- Source: Atwood WB et al. Astrophys J 2009;697:1071. arXiv:0902.1089. <https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.1089>
- Quote: "384 56-um wide aluminum readout strips spaced at 228 um pitch"
- Values: `stripPitchUm` = 228

**V38**. A 2026 study with an accelerator muon beam and a thorax phantom argues that the natural muon flux on Earth is too low for medical imaging.

- Status: verified (preprint)
- Source: Banerjee et al. arXiv:2610.16647 (2026). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16647>
- Quote: "The natural cosmic-ray muon flux is far too low for practical medical imaging"

### Countermeasures

**V41**. Neuromuscular electrical stimulation and blood-flow-restricted exercise have been proposed for spaceflight, but we found no published in-flight results.

- Status: secondary (secondary-citation)
- Source: Axiom Space, Muscle Stimulation research page (ESA study, ongoing); Hackney KJ et al. Extrem Physiol Med 2012 (PMC3710201). Read as search summaries only. <https://www.axiomspace.com/research/muscle-stimulation>
- Quote: none recorded
